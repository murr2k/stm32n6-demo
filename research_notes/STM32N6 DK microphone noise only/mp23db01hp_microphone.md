## 1. Clock ranges for power-down, low-power and performance modes; what should the output be at 768 kHz; is "digital silence at 768 kHz" consistent with a healthy part?

### Takeaway
No. 768 kHz is the datasheet's typical low-power-mode clock (range 0.54 to 1.1 MHz), where a healthy part outputs a live PDM stream with HIGHER sensitivity than performance mode (-24 dBFS versus -41 dBFS at 94 dB SPL). A constant output at 768 kHz does not match the datasheet; 384 kHz falls in an unspecified gap (0.15 to 0.54 MHz), so nothing can be concluded from that one. The clock table is identical in Rev 2 and Rev 4.

### Cited Findings
- fCLK ranges, Rev 4 Table 2 "Acoustic and electrical characteristics" (min / typ / max, MHz): power-down 0 / - / 0.15; low-power 0.54 / 0.768 / 1.1; performance 1.5 / 2.4 / 3.3. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2, p.3](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Rev 2 Table 3 "General microphone specifications" gives the same three ranges (0 to 0.15; 0.54 / 0.768 / 1.1; 1.5 / 2.4 / 3.3 MHz). - [DocID034289 Rev 2, Table 3, p.4](https://cdn.components.ru/media/PDF/1367796.pdf)
- The ranges 0.15 to 0.54 MHz and 1.1 to 1.5 MHz are not assigned to any mode in either revision; the datasheet says nothing about behaviour there. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Footnote 2 to fCLK: "Duty cycle: min = 40% max = 60%". Footnote 3: "In case of mode change (from low-power to performance mode or vice versa), the clock has to be continuous or has to be stopped for at least 50 us." - Figure 6 "Typical Idd vs. Vdd" (read off the plot, approximate): at 3.072 MHz about 1.0 to 1.08 mA across 1.6 to 3.6 V; 2.4 MHz about 0.9 to 0.95 mA; 1.536 MHz about 0.75 to 0.8 mA; 0.768 MHz about 0.32 to 0.37 mA; 0 MHz about 0. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figure 6, p.6](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Frequency response: -3 dB roll-off at 35 Hz; Figure 3 shows low-power and normal mode responses overlapping, flat from about 100 Hz to 5 kHz with a rise of about 2 dB at 10 kHz. Figure 4 "Typical PSRR": normal mode about 62 dB at 100 Hz rising to about 100 dB at 10 kHz; low-power about 53 dB rising to about 75 dB. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figures 3 and 4, p.6](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- A second, independent report on the same board model (STM32N6570-DK, on-board MP23DB01HP, MDF1): "R = 16 with a PDM clock near 1 MHz" (1.024 MHz, inside the low-power range) gave "mostly silence (about -71 dBFS) with loud bursts", while the roughly 2 MHz configurations gave audio. ST's two replies did not address this. - [ST Community thread 168802, 7 to 13 Oct 2026](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)

### Inferences
- Per the datasheet a healthy part clocked at 768 kHz (40 to 60 % duty, 20 ms after the first edge) is in low-power mode and must toggle; its audio is 17 dB hotter than in performance mode. Constant output there means one of: the part is not entering low-power mode (damaged or not the part the datasheet describes), the clock at the pin is not what was programmed (duty cycle outside 40 to 60 %, e.g. from an odd divider, or a different frequency), or the capture was taken inside the wake-up window. The 384 kHz result is uninformative (unspecified band).
- Two unrelated users of this board model now report no usable output in the low-power clock range (constant at 768 kHz here; near-silence with bursts at 1.024 MHz in the forum). That points at something common to the board or its clock generation rather than one damaged part, but it is only two data points and neither was explained by ST.
- Supply current is a cheap mode discriminator that does not depend on the data path: about 1 mA at 3.072 MHz, about 0.35 mA at 768 kHz, a few uA in power-down (Figure 6). If the board has a series element in the microphone's VDD, the voltage across it shows which mode the ASIC is actually in.
- The observed "same level from 1.5 to 3.2 MHz" does not discriminate: a healthy part's noise in dBFS is also nearly constant across performance mode (SNR 65 versus 65.5 dB).

### Gaps
- Behaviour in the unspecified bands (0.15 to 0.54 MHz, 1.1 to 1.5 MHz) is not documented in either revision.
- DOUT state in power-down (driven constant, or high impedance) is not documented.
- Rev 1 (April 2020) and Rev 3 (February 2022) were not found; their content is known only from the Rev 4 revision history. The "Rev 2 and Rev 4 are known to differ" premise checks out only for wake-up time (see section 4), the added performance curves, and document layout; the clock table did not change.
- Sigma-delta modulator order, clock jitter tolerance and recommended decimation filter: the datasheet is silent on all three. AN5027 (which may cover decimation) could not be read.

## 2. With L/R tied high, during which clock phase is DOUT driven; tDV and high-impedance timing; is "bit changes about 100 ns after the rising edge and stays for the whole cycle" consistent?

### Takeaway
Yes, consistent. With L/R = Vdd the part drives DOUT while CLK is high and releases it after the falling edge; data becomes valid at most 120 ns after the rising edge (100 pF load). A lone microphone on the line leaves it floating during CLK low, so the last bit persisting for the whole cycle is expected and is not evidence of a fault.

### Cited Findings
- Table 7 "L/R channel selection": L/R = GND: CLK low = data valid, CLK high = high impedance. L/R = Vdd: CLK low = high impedance, CLK high = data valid. Same table (Table 8) in Rev 2. - [DS13336 Rev 4, section 4.1, p.8](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf); [Rev 2, p.9](https://cdn.components.ru/media/PDF/1367796.pdf)
- Table 5 "Timing characteristics": Tdv, delay time to valid data (Cload = 100 pF): max 120 ns. Ten, delay time to driven data: min 19 ns. Tdis, delay time to high-Z: min 4 ns, max 17 ns. Identical in Rev 2 Table 6. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 5, p.5](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Figure 2 "Timing waveforms" (rendered and inspected): for L/R = Vdd, Ten and Tdv are measured from the rising CLK edge and Tdis from the falling edge; for L/R = GND the same from the opposite edges. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figure 2, p.5](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- "The L/R pin must be connected to Vdd or GND"; the pin has an internal 200 kOhm pull-down to GND. Logic thresholds: VIL max 0.35 x Vdd, VIH min 0.65 x Vdd. - [DS13336 Rev 4, section 4.1 and Table 2](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- DOUT load capacitance: 100 pF max. Short-circuit current 1 to 10 mA. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- ST's own BSP for this board samples the microphone on the falling clock edge: `hmdf->Init.FilterBistream = MDF_BITSTREAM0_FALLING`, serial interface `MDF_SITF_NORMAL_SPI_MODE`, clock source `MDF_SITF_CCK0_SOURCE`. - [stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c, lines 2791 to 2794](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6570-dk-bsp/blob/main/stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c)

### Inferences
- A 10 kOhm pull-up against the internal 200 kOhm pull-down puts L/R at 200/210 = 0.952 x Vdd (3.14 V at 3.3 V), comfortably above 0.65 x Vdd. The datasheet wording is "connected to", not "pulled to", but the level is valid.
- The correct sampling point for L/R = Vdd is the falling edge (data has been valid for most of the high phase and is held at least 4 ns after the edge). Sampling on the rising edge reads the previous bit held by line capacitance, which also works for a single microphone, so either edge returns a valid stream; edge choice cannot turn sound into noise here.
- At 3.072 MHz the high phase is 163 ns at 50 % duty; with Tdv up to 120 ns the margin before the falling edge is at least 43 ns, less if the duty cycle is below 50 %. An observed 100 ns is within specification.

### Gaps
- No setup/hold figures other than Tdv, Ten and Tdis are given; Tdv has no minimum and Ten has no maximum.
- The datasheet does not say whether a pull resistor on DOUT is advisable for a single-microphone bus.
- Section 5 says the decoupling capacitors (100 nF and 1 uF ceramic) go "as near as possible to pin 2", but pin 2 is L/R and VDD is pin 5 (Table 1, Figure 9): a datasheet error, present in Rev 4.

## 3. Expected idle noise in dBFS; level of normal speech at 30 cm and of a 70 dB SPL tone; digital gain applied by reference designs

### Takeaway
Performance-mode idle noise is about -106.5 dBFS(A) (equivalent input noise 28.5 dB(A) SPL) and full scale is 135 dB SPL, so a 70 dB SPL tone sits at about -65 dBFS: with no make-up gain it is 18 LSB rms in 16-bit PCM. The reported noise, 40 to 50 dB above the floor, is therefore about -66 to -56 dBFS, the level a 68 to 78 dB SPL sound would produce. ST's BSP for this board applies no microphone-specific make-up gain that could be confirmed, which fits forum reports of "very faint" audio from healthy-looking setups.

### Cited Findings
- Sensitivity -41 dBFS (+/-1 dB) at 94 dB SPL, 1 kHz; SNR 65.5 dB(A) at 3.072 MHz, 65 dB(A) at 2.4 MHz; AOP 135 dB SPL (performance mode). - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 4](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Low-power mode: -24 dBFS, SNR 64 dB(A) over 20 Hz to 8 kHz, AOP 120 dB SPL. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 3](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Figure 8 "Noise floor power spectral density", unit dB(FS^2/Hz), read off the rendered plot (approximate, +/-3 dB): performance mode (2.4 and 3.072 MHz curves overlap) about -103 at 20 Hz, about -125 to -130 at 100 Hz, about -140 at 1 kHz, about -143 to -145 from 3 kHz to 20 kHz. Low-power (0.768 MHz) about -85 at 20 Hz, about -118 at 100 Hz, about -125 from 1 kHz to 8 kHz with an upturn near 10 kHz. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figure 8, p.6](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Figure 5 "Typical THD at 1 kHz vs. sound pressure level": normal mode crosses 1 % near 120 dB SPL and reaches about 5 % near 134 dB SPL; low-power mode rises steeply past 10 % at about 120 dB SPL. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figure 5](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- ST BSP MDF settings for the on-board microphone (`MDF_*` macros): CIC Sinc4 at every rate; CIC decimation 64 (8 k), 32 (16 k), 16 (32 k, 48 k), 8 (96 k); reshape filter enabled with a further decimation by 4; `Gain` -4 (8 k), +2 (16 k), +10 (32/44.1/48 k), +18 (96 k); high-pass enabled at 0.000625 x fPCM; `Offset` 0; `DiscardSamples` 0. Clock: PLL3 from HSE (48 MHz), IC8, MDF1 kernel clock; output clock divider 12 at 16 k and 8 at 48 k. - [stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c, lines 151 to 200 and 2721 to 2818](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6570-dk-bsp/blob/main/stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c)
- A forum user describes the BSP 16 kHz defaults as "Sinc4, R = 32, reshape on, /4 (total decimation 128)", "Gain: +2 (about +6 dB)", PDM clock about 2.08 MHz, and found "Sinc5 at gain -8 matched Sinc4 at +2 in loudness". - [ST Community thread 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)
- ST employee (JonathanC, 13 Oct 2026): "There is no official configuration, it depends on the use case targeted", pointing to X-CUBE-AUDIO-KIT and AN5795 for example settings for this microphone. - [same thread](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)
- Other users of the on-board microphone with ST's BSP report: "I hear mostly noise; if I tap near the microphone, a barely audible sound comes through" and that they "have to actually blow into it" (velfinnix, 23 Sep 2026, STM32Cube_FW_N6_V1.4.1, same code fine on STM32H7S78-DK); "Captured audio is just noise" and "the buffer is modified but contains random noise which changes every callback" (grashnar, Dec 2025, board revision B, STM32CubeN6 v1.3.0). Neither thread reached a root cause. - [thread 167402](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6-nucleo-board-mdf-issue-mics-records-noise-167402); [thread 159675](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mic-and-screen-setup-159675)

### Inferences
- Idle noise = sensitivity minus SNR: -41 - 65.5 = -106.5 dBFS(A) at 3.072 MHz (-106 at 2.4 MHz); low-power -24 - 64 = -88 dBFS(A). Equivalent input noise 94 - 65.5 = 28.5 dB(A) SPL (30 dB(A) in low-power).
- 0 dBFS corresponds to 94 + 41 = 135 dB SPL in performance mode (equal to the AOP) and 118 dB SPL in low-power mode.
- 70 dB SPL tone: -41 - 24 = -65 dBFS rms. Unity gain: 18.4 LSB rms in 16-bit, about 4700 LSB rms in 24-bit. To put 70 dB SPL at -25 dBFS takes about 40 dB of digital gain; the AOP headroom is what costs the level.
- Speech at 30 cm: assuming the usual 60 dB SPL at 1 m for conversational speech (assumption, not sourced here), inverse-square gives about 70 dB SPL at 30 cm, so about -65 dBFS rms with peaks roughly 12 to 15 dB higher.
- Observed noise: -106.5 + (40 to 50) = about -66.5 to -56.5 dBFS, i.e. equivalent to 68 to 78 dB SPL broadband. A 70 dB SPL tone is then comparable to the total noise but must still stand out in a narrowband spectrum: the noise in a 10 Hz bin is about 33 dB below the 20 kHz total. Tapping the package or board produces well over 100 dB SPL at the port (above -35 dBFS). "No response to tapping" is therefore the strong observation; it cannot be a level or gain problem.
- A healthy part's noise spectrum is not a uniform 2 dB/octave: Figure 8 falls by roughly 35 to 40 dB between 20 Hz and 1 kHz and is flat above about 3 kHz. A noise floor 40 to 50 dB up with a gentle uniform slope is a different source from the normal one scaled up.
- BSP gain: the `Gain` steps track the CIC gain exactly (Sinc4, R 32 to 16 is a factor 2^4 = 24.1 dB; `Gain` +2 to +10 is 8 steps of about 3 dB = 24 dB; the forum's Sinc5/-8 versus Sinc4/+2 equivalence is the same arithmetic). So the BSP normalises the filter's own gain across sample rates and there is no sign of extra gain added for a -41 dBFS microphone. That matches "very faint, have to blow into it" from users whose data path otherwise works.

### Gaps
- The absolute net gain of the BSP chain (PDM full scale to PCM full scale) was not derived: it needs the MDF data-path widths from the reference manual (RM0486) or AN5795, neither read here.
- X-CUBE-AUDIO-KIT and X-CUBE-MEMSMIC1 gain settings for this microphone were not retrieved.
- The A-weighted noise figure and the Figure 8 density do not reconcile exactly (flat -143 dB(FS^2/Hz) over 20 kHz integrates to about -100 dBFS unweighted); plot-reading error and the dBFS reference convention probably account for it. Treat the absolute floor as -100 to -107 dBFS.
- The datasheet does not define the THD criterion for AOP.

## 4. Documented start-up requirements that, if violated, leave the part outputting noise (VDD ramp versus clock, clock before VDD, clock-stop time on mode change, wake-up time)

### Takeaway
The datasheet documents only three things: a wake-up time from the first clock edge (10 ms in Rev 2, 20 ms in Rev 4), a 40 to 60 % clock duty cycle, and "clock continuous or stopped for at least 50 us" when changing between low-power and performance mode. It contains no VDD ramp, no VDD-versus-clock sequencing rule and no description of any latched bad state, so a permanent noise-only condition cannot be attributed to a documented start-up violation.

### Cited Findings
- Wake-up time TWK, Rev 4: 20 ms, "Specified by design", footnote 5 "Time from the first clock edge to valid output data". - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2, p.3](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Wake-up time TWK, Rev 2: 10 ms, "Guaranteed by design", same footnote. - [DocID034289 Rev 2, Table 3, p.4](https://cdn.components.ru/media/PDF/1367796.pdf)
- Revision history: Rev 2 (27 Jul 2020) "Added footnote 3 regarding the clock"; Rev 3 (10 Feb 2022) "Updated wake-up time", replaced the frequency-response section with typical performance curves; Rev 4 (7 Mar 2022) updated Figure 3. - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 9, p.13](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- ST employee (Emmanuel_C, 13 Oct 2026) reads the Rev 4 figure as a maximum: per the datasheet "the maximum wake-up time is 20 ms". - [ST Community thread 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)
- Mode change: "the clock has to be continuous or has to be stopped for at least 50 us" (Table 2 footnote 3, both revisions). Duty cycle 40 to 60 % (footnote 2). - [DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Decoupling: 100 nF plus 1 uF ceramic close to the supply pin; "Bypass capacitor of 200 nF or 1 uF (better) is highly recommended for ESD main clamp integrity". Input voltage on any control pin: -0.3 to Vdd + 0.3 V (absolute maximum). - [DS13336 Rev 4, section 5 and Table 6](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Related field observation on this board: with a 10 Hz high-pass in the MDF a user saw a DC offset of "about 0.04 of full scale" and a start-up transient 10 to 12 dB louder for 0.5 s decaying over about 4 s; ST attributed it to high-pass convergence and recommended 40 Hz (95 % in 12 ms, 99 % in 20 ms). - [ST Community thread 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)

### Inferences
- The only sequencing constraint that can be derived is from the absolute maximum table: CLK must not exceed Vdd + 0.3 V, so a clock driven into an unpowered part violates an absolute maximum rating. That is a damage mechanism (input clamp current), not a documented "noise state". On the DK the microphone's VDD is a fixed 3.3 V rail, so this applies only if that rail is switched or late.
- Sweeping the clock from 3.072 MHz to 768 kHz without either keeping it continuous or stopping it for 50 us is the one documented way to mis-select a mode. A firmware that reprograms the divider and gates the clock for less than 50 us fits that description; stopping the clock for a few milliseconds between settings removes the question.
- Wake-up: discard at least 20 ms (61 440 clocks at 3.072 MHz) after the first edge before judging the stream. A capture that is noise-only seconds later is not a wake-up effect.

### Gaps
- VDD ramp rate, VDD-before-clock or clock-before-VDD ordering, brown-out behaviour and any power-on-reset threshold: not in the datasheet (either revision).
- Clock jitter requirement: not in the datasheet.
- No ST document, erratum or forum answer was found that describes this part latching into a noise-only state.

## 5. What ST and other vendors say about damaged MEMS microphones: symptoms, noise spectrum, whether a damaged part still produces a normal-looking bitstream, how to confirm; handling and soldering cautions; port and PCB requirements

### Takeaway
Vendor documents agree on causes (contamination through the sound port, air blown into or pulled from the port, ultrasonic cleaning, board wash, shock) and name two symptoms, loss of sensitivity and noisy output, but none found here describes the bitstream of a dead sensor or gives a noise spectrum. ST's own note ties "noisy output or poor SNR" to membrane contamination and says a sensitivity check reveals a damaged membrane (with THD and noise floor out of specification as well), so the confirmation test is acoustic: a known SPL at the port and a comparison with -41 dBFS.

### Cited Findings
- ST AN4428 "Best practices in the manufacturing process of MEMS microphones" (DocID025706 Rev 1, January 2014, document DM00103202) (excerpt only): "The sensitivity can be used to determine if the membrane is damaged in which case the THD, as well as the noise floor, is out of specification." - [AN4428](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00103202.pdf)
- AN4428 (excerpt only): a noisy output or poor SNR "is typically due to contamination of the MEMS membrane"; contamination can occur during PCB sawing, PCB cleaning or washing, and during soldering (solder entering the sound inlet); sealing the sound inlet with foam or tape before such processing is recommended; a defined pick-up area avoids damage to the membrane. - [AN4428](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00103202-best-practices-in-the-manufacturing-process-of-mems-microphones-stmicroelectronics.pdf)
- A forum user cites AN4428 section 2.1.1 as stating that contaminated microphones can exhibit high noise output or degraded SNR, and describes his own case: 32-microphone array, some parts with "very high intermittent noise" that worsened over time, white-noise-shaped spectrum with strong components near DC (about 3 Hz), sound inlets "completely clogged with soldering flux", clocks checked and fine. - [ST Community, "MEMS microphones and contamination", Nov 2014](https://community.st.com/t5/mems-sensors/mems-microphones-and-contamination/m-p/470216)
- ST employee reply in that thread (Alessandro Morcelli, 1 Dec 2014): a clogged inlet gives "lower sensitivity"; flux reaching the MEMS itself causes "very unpredictable failure modes" depending on the size, weight and position of particles; he had seen contaminants cause "high noise only in presence of sound or in an intermittent way"; no chemical cleaning is recommended because "once the MEMS is contaminated with a substance like the flux, the device is definitely compromised". - [same thread](https://community.st.com/t5/mems-sensors/mems-microphones-and-contamination/m-p/470216)
- ST AN4426 "Tutorial for MEMS microphones" (DocID025704 Rev 2, February 2017) (excerpt only): careful soldering is required to keep dust or solder paste out of the sound port, which would damage the MEMS membrane. - [AN4426](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00103199-tutorial-for-mems-microphones-stmicroelectronics.pdf)
- MP23DB01HP datasheet cautions: "This device is sensitive to mechanical shock, improper handling can cause permanent damage to the part" and the same sentence for ESD (HBM 2 kV, MM 200 V, CDM 750 V, 8 kV IEC 61000-4-2 contact to housing). - [DS13336 Rev 4, section 3, p.7](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Reflow (Table 8, Pb-free, JEDEC J-STD-020): ramp 3 C/s max; preheat 150 to 200 C for 60 to 120 s; above 217 C for 60 to 150 s; peak 260 C max, 20 to 40 s within 5 C of peak; ramp-down 6 C/s max; 25 C to peak 8 minutes max. - [DS13336 Rev 4, section 6.1, p.10](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- Package and port: RHLGA 5-lead metal cap, 3.5 x 2.65 x 0.98 mm (1.08 mm max height in the outline table), bottom port, EMI shielded. Pin 3 is a ground ring around the port. Land pattern (Figure 13): ring outer diameter 1.625 mm, inner 1.025 mm, PCB hole 0.5 mm; stencil splits the ring into two arcs with 0.3 mm gaps (so paste does not form a continuous ring). - [DS13336 Rev 4, Figures 12 and 13, p.11](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)
- InvenSense AN-100 "MEMS Microphone Handling and Assembly Guide" (excerpt only; cdiweb copy is Rev 1.1, TDK lists v1.5): do not pick up the microphone with a vacuum tool that contacts the sound port; do not pull air out of or blow air into the port; the microphone can be damaged by cleaning processes because solvents enter through the port hole; no ultrasonic baths; the port is sensitive to solder flux; no vapor-phase soldering; placement force above about 1 kg is excessive. - [AN-100, cdiweb](https://www.cdiweb.com/datasheets/invensense/MEMS-Microphone-Handling-Guide.pdf)
- TDK T5919 / INMP504 datasheet handling sections (excerpt only): keep water away from the port when washing the PCB; "do not use blow-off procedures or ultrasonic cleaning"; no vacuum tool on the port side. - [T5919 handling instructions](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/2889916/Tdk-T5919.html?page=19); [INMP504 datasheet](https://www.alldatasheet.com/html-pdf/1140862/TDK/INMP504ACEZ-R0/829/11/INMP504ACEZ-R0.html)
- InvenSense AN-1124 on sealing bottom-port microphones (excerpt only): liquid ingress can permanently damage a microphone. - [AN-1124](https://www.mouser.lt/pdfDocs/Recommendations-for-Sealing-InvenSense-Bottom-Port-3.pdf)
- Patent literature on the failure itself: diaphragm damage from overload, mechanical stress or electrostatic charge causes "partial or total loss" of acoustic sensitivity, and conventional test applies sound and treats no response as a torn membrane ([US 11632639](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11632639)); a pressure impulse at the port can make the membrane contact the substrate and stick (stiction), degrading or killing the part ([US 10450189](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10450189)); particles or vapour in the back volume hinder diaphragm movement and reduce sensitivity ([US 11533565](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11533565)).
- Reliability study (Microelectronics Reliability, 2014): shock-test failures were cracking of the diaphragm, backplate and runner; mixed-flowing-gas exposure embrittled the membrane through surface oxide growth. - [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0026271414000079)

### Inferences
- The sigma-delta modulator is in the ASIC and runs from the clock regardless of the transducer, so a part with a ruptured, stuck or disconnected membrane will still emit a well-formed, noise-shaped 1-bit stream. No source found states this outright; it follows from the architecture (capacitive sensor plus CMOS interface IC, datasheet p.1) and from the patent's description of dead parts being detected acoustically rather than electrically.
- What the vendor material does NOT support is the specific signature here. A torn or stuck membrane is documented as loss of sensitivity; contamination is documented as noise that is intermittent, grows over time or appears with sound. A steady Gaussian floor 40 to 50 dB above normal with zero acoustic response is not described in any source found. If the sensor were simply disconnected, the remaining ASIC noise would be expected at or below the datasheet floor, not far above it (reasoning, unsourced). Elevated steady noise needs a noise source at the ASIC's high-impedance input (leakage from contamination or moisture, or a damaged bias/charge-pump circuit) or an origin outside the microphone.
- The forum evidence weighs against a single damaged part: at least three other users of the STM32N6570-DK report noise-only or near-inaudible capture from the on-board microphone with ST's own BSP and examples, across board revision B and firmware packages 1.3.0 and 1.4.1, and one of them hears a faint response to tapping. Either the DK's microphone circuit has a common weakness (a batch of parts, the assembly process, or the board's clock/supply), or the common factor is the unity-gain BSP plus an unusually high noise floor. A dead U13 on one board remains possible but is no longer the default explanation.
- Confirmation tests that do not rely on the host data path: (a) supply current versus clock (section 1); (b) scope DOUT while tapping the board: a live sensor visibly changes the bit density during the tap at 135 dB SPL full scale; (c) swap in a known-good PDM microphone on the same nets (the DK's CN5 accepts STEVAL-MIC008A per [thread 158093](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/interleave-audio-by-connecting-steval-mic008a-dual-mems-to-stm32n6570-dk-cn5-158093)); (d) a calibrated 94 dB SPL, 1 kHz source at the port should read -41 dBFS +/-1 dB: per AN4428 the sensitivity check is the damaged-membrane test.
- Light sensitivity: the bottom-port metal-cap package faces the PCB; nothing suggests a light path, and the datasheet does not raise it.

### Gaps
- AN4428, AN4426, AN4427 (gasket design), AN4211 (soldering guidelines) and AN5027 could not be opened; section numbers and exact wording beyond the excerpts are unverified. AN4428's text on air blow, vacuum, ultrasonic and wash was not retrieved.
- No Infineon (AN557, AN558) or Knowles (SiSonic design guide) text on damaged-microphone symptoms was retrieved; the searches returned only their titles.
- No source gives the noise spectrum of a damaged or contaminated MEMS microphone, and none states what a collapsed-diaphragm part's PDM stream looks like. No source discusses charge-pump failure symptoms.
- Light sensitivity, gasket and acoustic-path requirements for this part: the datasheet is silent (it only gives the 0.5 mm PCB hole in the land pattern).
- The DK's actual PCB hole, any membrane or label over it, and its assembly process (wash, conformal steps) are unknown.

## 6. Is the part NRND; errata, PCNs or known lot issues

### Takeaway
The MP23DB01HP is Not Recommended for New Design, confirmed by an ST employee in October 2026. No erratum, PCN or lot advisory was found, but there is a cluster of unresolved ST Community reports (late 2025 to October 2026) of the STM32N6570-DK's on-board microphone giving noise only or barely audible audio.

### Inferences
- NRND status by itself says nothing about quality; it does mean ST is unlikely to publish new characterisation or errata for the part.
- The recurrence of "noise only" from different users, firmware versions and at least one DK revision makes a board-level or lot-level cause worth ruling out before concluding that one microphone was damaged in handling. One user did get correct audio at about 2 MHz on this board model, so the design can work.
