## Is there any public project demonstrating good audio (speech recognition, KWS, AED, recording) from the STM32N6570-DK onboard microphone, and with what exact configuration?

### Takeaway
Yes, two independent non-ST-Cube code bases get intelligible speech from the stock onboard microphone on MDF1 (CCK0/SDI0 on PE2/PE8): a third-party free-speech recognizer (fotonik-sk, HAL-based, `Gain = 16`, SINC4/32, reshape /4, MSB-only 16-bit) and the upstream Zephyr MDF DMIC driver with the `stm32n6570_dk` board devicetree (merged September 2026, after Zephyr 4.4.0). Both use the same filter topology as ST's own code; the differences are almost entirely in gain and in which 16 bits of the 24-bit result are kept.

### Cited Findings

**fotonik-sk/stm32n6-speech-recognition (third party, Apache-2.0 code, created 2026-10-04, 46 stars)**
- README: "On the STM32N6570-DK board it listens to the on-board microphone and prints English text on the display as you speak"; "Besides public speech datasets, the model was trained on 248 hours of recordings made through this board's microphone"; "Recognition works best within about one meter of the microphone." Demo video linked in the README. - [README.md](https://github.com/fotonik-sk/stm32n6-speech-recognition/blob/main/README.md)

Comment: "The microphone clock on CCK0 is the 49.14 MHz IC8 kernel clock / 2 (processing clock) / 12 = about 2.048 MHz. Comment: "32 x 4 = 128 turns the 2.048 MHz bitstream into about 16 kHz PCM. ### Inferences
- The fotonik firmware is the strongest public proof that a stock DK onboard microphone delivers usable speech: one metre working distance, 248 h of recordings, and an ST employee acknowledging it. Its configuration is ST's `MDF_AudioRecorder` topology with `Gain` raised from 14 to 16 and an HSI-sourced PLL3.
- The fotonik pin labels (PE8 = clock, PE2 = data) and the "MP34DT06J" part name are almost certainly comment errors: both pins are configured identically as AF4 push-pull, so which one is CCK0 is decided by the chip's alternate-function map, and ST's BSP and Zephyr agree on PE2 = CCK0, PE8 = SDI0. The part-number comment conflicts with the MP23DB01HP that forum users and the task brief name for U13; I did not check a schematic.
- Neither working implementation applies a hardware step (solder bridge, jumper) or a start-up delay beyond the HAL init sequence.

### Gaps
- Board revision used by fotonik and by the Zephyr tester (kartben) is not stated in either source, so it is unknown whether they ran on MB1939 rev C02.
- No published level measurement (dBFS for a known SPL) exists for any of these projects.
- NanoEdge AI datalogging firmware for the N6 DK, Hackster, Hackaday and YouTube projects: none surfaced in the searches run; not searched exhaustively.
- `amurzeau/damc_stm32` (USB audio DSP, has a `src/stm32n6570-dk` port with a BSP audio copy), `camthink-ai/ne301` (different N6 board with a BSP copy) and `d72gq2sj52-cmd/MasterProjektSS26_Audio` (student copy of ST's GettingStarted-Audio including the Patch folder) were found by code search but not read in enough depth to say which input they use or how well it works.

## Do ST's audio AI demos for the N6 DK (speech enhancement, audio event detection) use the onboard microphone, and do their READMEs mention expected level, required gain, or a hardware setup step?

### Takeaway
Yes: ST's STM32N6-GettingStarted-Audio (AED and speech enhancement; the same code is what stm32ai-modelzoo-services deploys from `application_code/audio/STM32N6`) and the newer x-cube-n6-ai-vad record from the onboard digital microphone through BSP audio-in instance 1 (the MDF path) at 16 kHz, 16-bit, mono. The READMEs give no expected level, no gain guidance and no microphone-specific hardware step; the notable thing is that ST does not use the stock BSP file but a patched copy (HSI-sourced PLL4, non-cacheable and 32-byte-aligned DMA buffer and nodes).

### Inferences
- ST's own application teams consistently avoid two things in the stock BSP: HSE as the audio PLL source (every ST application found uses HSI for the MDF PLL) and a cacheable DMA target/node. On a system with the data cache on, a cacheable `Audio_DigMicRecBuff` without invalidation would return stale buffer contents, which is one known way to get "data that does not follow the sound"; the patch is ST's fix for their demo configuration. Whether either matters for a Zephyr build depends on that build's cache and clock setup.
- The `Volume` field and `BSP_AUDIO_IN_SetVolume` do nothing for the digital microphone, so anyone tuning "volume" through the BSP is not changing gain.
- `CTRL_X_CUBE_AI_SENSOR_FS (112.5F)` is presumably the acoustic level in dB SPL that the application treats as digital full scale; the README excerpt does not define it, so this reading is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- The READMEs do not state an expected dBFS level for speech, a noise floor, or a pass/fail check for the microphone.
- I did not read the STM32N6-GettingStarted-Audio README in raw form (only a fetched summary), nor the stm32ai-modelzoo-services audio deployment README; the latter was only confirmed by code search to contain `application_code/audio/STM32N6` copies in third-party forks.
- Why ST switched the audio PLLs from HSE to HSI is not documented in the patch or README.

## What gain do these projects use? Do any apply 30 to 45 dB of digital gain?

### Takeaway
Yes. Every DK implementation applies far more gain than a "full-scale PDM fits in full-scale PCM" setting: explicit MDF `Gain` of 14 (ST example, about +42 dB) or 16 (fotonik, about +48 dB) with MSB-aligned 16-bit output, or a small MDF gain (2 in the ST BSP, about 1 in Zephyr) followed by keeping the low 16 bits of the 24-bit sample with saturation, which is another +48 dB. By my arithmetic that is roughly 36 to 48 dB above the no-clip scale for SINC4/32 with the reshape filter.

### Cited Findings
- HAL definition: `int32_t Gain; /*!< Filter gain in step of around 3db (from -48db to 72dB). This parameter must be a number between Min_Data = -16 and Max_Data = 24 */`; `#define IS_MDF_GAIN(PARAM) ((-16 <= (PARAM)) && ((PARAM) <= 24))`. - [stm32n6xx_hal_mdf.h](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/hal_stm32/blob/main/stm32cube/stm32n6xx/drivers/include/stm32n6xx_hal_mdf.h)
- HAL `MsbOnly`: the DMA source address becomes `&hmdf->Instance->DFLTDR + 2U`, i.e. the upper 16 bits of the left-aligned 24-bit result. - [stm32n6xx_hal_mdf.c](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/hal_stm32/blob/main/stm32cube/stm32n6xx/drivers/src/stm32n6xx_hal_mdf.c)
- SCALE code to dB table used by the Zephyr driver (ST-authored): 0x00 = 0 dB, 0x01 = 3.5, 0x02 = 6.0, 0x08 = 24.1, 0x0E = 42.1, 0x10 = 48.2, 0x18 = 72.2 dB; negative side 0x20 = -48.2 to 0x2F = -2.5 dB. - [dmic_stm32_mdf.c](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/blob/main/drivers/audio/dmic_stm32_mdf.c)
- No-saturation rule in the same driver: "max scale = 20 * log10( 2 exp DS / D exp NF ) - DS = max data size at scale output (RSFLT on: DS = 22 / RSFLT off: DS = 24) - NF = Main CIC filter order", and "The CIC output data resolution cannot exceed 26 bits"; maximum CIC decimation per order 0 to 5: `{512, 512, 512, 322, 76, 32}`. - [dmic_stm32_mdf.c](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/blob/main/drivers/audio/dmic_stm32_mdf.c)
- ST `MDF_AudioRecorder`: `MdfFilterConfig0.Gain = 14;` with SINC4, `DecimationRatio = 32`, reshape /4, HPF `MDF_HPF_CUTOFF_0_000625FPCM`, `dma_config.MsbOnly = ENABLE`, `ProcClockDivider = 2`, `OutputClock.Divider = 12`, `MDF_SITF_NORMAL_SPI_MODE`, `MDF_BITSTREAM0_FALLING`, `Threshold = 31`; PLL3 from HSI with M 8, N 172, P1 7, P2 4. - [MDF_AudioRecorder main.c](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/STM32CubeN6/blob/main/Projects/STM32N6570-DK/Examples/MDF/MDF_AudioRecorder/FSBL/Src/main.c)
- fotonik: `filter.Gain = 16U;`, `MsbOnly = ENABLE`, no later software gain. - [drv_pdm_mdf.c](https://github.com/fotonik-sk/stm32n6-speech-recognition/blob/main/src/hardware/drivers/pdm/mdf/drv_pdm_mdf.c)
- ST BSP (and therefore the AED/SE/VAD demos): `MDF_GAIN` = 2 at 16 kHz, `MsbOnly = DISABLE`, `tmp = Audio_DigMicRecBuff[index] / 256; tmp = SaturaLH(tmp, -32768, 32767);`. - [stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6570-dk-bsp/blob/main/stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c)
- Zephyr: `flt_cfg->Gain = scale;` is set from the computed maximum scale on every `configure()`; in the ISR path `sample >>= MDF_DFLTDR_DR_Pos;` then `sys_put_le16((uint16_t)CLAMP(sample, INT16_MIN, INT16_MAX), dst)` for 16-bit streams. A review comment on the PR notes that the devicetree `gain` property "never reaches the hardware" because it is overwritten. - [dmic_stm32_mdf.c](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/blob/main/drivers/audio/dmic_stm32_mdf.c), [zephyr PR 112277](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/pull/112277)
- OpenMV N6 (different board and microphone): default `gain_db = 24`, `Gain = gain_db / 3` (8), `>> 16`. - [py_audio.c](https://github.com/openmv/openmv/blob/master/ports/stm32/modules/py_audio.c)
- ST employee guidance on the forum (2026-10-13): "there is no official configuration"; the right choice depends on near/far field and output width; pointers to X-CUBE-AUDIO-KIT and AN5795; `LF_MASTER_SPI` is only needed for low power. A second ST employee: MP23 microphones are "Not Recommended for New Design", maximum wake-up time 20 ms from the datasheet, and the first-order HPF needs settling time (10 Hz cutoff: 95% in 48 ms, 99% in 80 ms; 40 Hz: 95% in 12 ms, 99% in 20 ms), recommending 40 Hz over 10 Hz. - [ST Community thread 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)
- The same thread's original poster (vedantgg, 2026-10-07, DK with MP23DB01HP on MDF1 filter 0, PE2/PE8) reported that a decimation of 16 with an about 1 MHz PDM clock "gave mostly silence", a start-up transient with the 10 Hz HPF, and repeated `BSP_AUDIO_IN_DeInit`/`Init` failures. - [ST Community thread 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)

### Inferences
(All my arithmetic from the cited values; "16-bit output level" is relative to SCALE = 0 dB read MSB-aligned.)

| Implementation | MDF SCALE | Extra from bit selection | Total vs SCALE 0 / MSB-aligned | Above the no-clip scale for SINC4, D = 32, reshape on (max 6.0 dB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ST MDF_AudioRecorder | 14 = +42.1 dB | 0 (MsbOnly) | +42 dB | about +36 dB |
| fotonik ASR | 16 = +48.2 dB | 0 (MsbOnly) | +48 dB | about +42 dB |
| ST BSP at 16 kHz (AED, SE, VAD demos) | 2 = +6.0 dB | +48.2 dB (low 16 of 24, saturating) | +54 dB | about +48 dB |
| Zephyr at `pcm_width` 16, D = 32 | 0x01 = +3.5 dB (largest table entry strictly below 6.0) | +48.2 dB (24-bit sample clamped to int16) | about +52 dB | about +46 dB |
| OpenMV N6 default (other board) | 8 = +24.1 dB | 0 (`>> 16`) | +24 dB | about +18 dB |

- So a capture that uses a no-clip scale (HAL `Gain` 0 to 2) and keeps the upper 16 bits will sit 36 to 54 dB below what all of the working DK implementations deliver. With that setup, ordinary speech would be buried near the quantization floor of the 16-bit word; this is consistent with the vedantgg report of "mostly silence" at a low-gain setting.
- The spread between working projects (42 to 54 dB total) means none of them is at a carefully calibrated optimum; they all simply trade about 6 to 8 bits of headroom for level, and loud sources will clip.
- Gain alone cannot explain a capture that shows noise and no response at all to sound: with too little gain the signal is small but still correlated with sound. The gain comparison is necessary context, not a diagnosis.

### Gaps
- The exact gain of the reshape filter and the mapping from the 22-bit scale output to the 24-bit result are taken from the Zephyr driver's comments, not from the reference manual; the "above no-clip" column could be off by a few dB.
- No project states the microphone sensitivity it assumed, and none publishes a measured level.
- Zephyr's actual PDM clock and CIC decimation on the DK depend on the IC8 rate and on the application's `min_pdm_clk_freq`/`max_pdm_clk_freq`; I did not run it, so the D = 32 row is illustrative.

## Are there Zephyr issues or PRs about the N6 DK microphone or an MDF DMIC driver, and what state are they in?

### Takeaway
An ST-authored MDF DMIC driver (`drivers/audio/dmic_stm32_mdf.c`, compatible `st,stm32-mdf`) with STM32N6570-DK board support (alias `dmic0`, PE2/PE8, IC8 from PLL3) was merged upstream on 2026-09-03 and is listed in the 4.5 release notes; a clamp bug that made 24-bit capture a full-scale square wave on the DK was fixed on 2026-09-14. It is interrupt-driven (no DMA), mono only, and has open roadmap items. It is not part of Zephyr 4.4.0.

### Cited Findings

"It provides support for STM32N6xx series, but the IP is available on H5/H7RS/MP2/N6/U3/U5". Test results: DMIC API suite 4 of 6 pass (`test_max_channel` and `test_stereo_channel` fail because more than one channel per filter is unsupported). ### Inferences
- The driver and board node were merged in September 2026 and appear in `doc/releases/release-notes-4.5.rst`, so a Zephyr 4.4.0 workspace does not have them; they could be taken as an out-of-tree reference or backport. The upstream node is the closest thing to a "known good" Zephyr configuration for this exact board: pins PE2/PE8, SINC4, reshape /4, HPF 0.000625 x Fpcm, kernel clock IC8 from PLL3.
- From the board file as written, PLL3 = 48 MHz / 3 x 125 = 2000 MHz and IC8 = PLL3 / 1, so the MDF kernel clock handed to the driver is very high and the driver must use a large PROCDIV x CCKDIV product (my arithmetic: 572 for a 3.5 MHz ceiling, giving about 3.497 MHz PDM, CIC decimation 55, about 15.9 kHz). This means the Zephyr path does not run the microphone at 2.048 MHz and still produced intelligible speech for the PR 118736 author. I did not verify the effective IC8 rate (the `hse-div2` property may or may not affect the PLL input), so treat these numbers as unconfirmed.
- Because the Zephyr driver takes one interrupt per PCM sample (16 000 per second) and has no DMA, it is a functional reference, not a model for a frame-pacing-sensitive application.
- The 24-bit clamp bug is a concrete example of a DK capture that "looked plausible in code" but produced garbage until the sample conversion was fixed; the fix was in sample handling, not in the MDF settings.

### Gaps
- Neither PR states the board revision, the measured level, or the PDM clock actually produced.
- Confirmed from the driver's device macro (not a gap, recorded here for completeness): `.SerialInterface = { .Activation = ENABLE, .Mode = MDF_SITF_NORMAL_SPI_MODE, .ClockSource = MDF_SITF_CCK0_SOURCE, .Threshold = 31 }`, `.FilterBistream = MDF_BITSTREAM<child index>_FALLING`, `.DataSource = MDF_DATA_SOURCE_BSMX`, `.OutputClock.Pins = MDF_OUTPUT_CLOCK_0`, `.AcquisitionMode = MDF_MODE_ASYNC_CONT`, `.FifoThreshold = MDF_FIFO_THRESHOLD_NOT_EMPTY`, `.Gain = DT gain / 3` (then overwritten in `configure()`). Source: [dmic_stm32_mdf.c](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/blob/main/drivers/audio/dmic_stm32_mdf.c)
- The bitstream edge is derived from the filter's child index, so a filter other than filter 0 would select a different bitstream input; not relevant to the DK node but worth knowing when adapting it.
- Whether anyone has run the Zephyr driver on MB1939 rev C02 specifically is unknown.
