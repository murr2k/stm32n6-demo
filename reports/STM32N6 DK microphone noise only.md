# Onboard microphone streams noise, hears nothing

The bench data clears the capture chain and places the problem on U13's side of PE8: the microphone emits a well-formed, noise-shaped PDM stream with datasheet-correct timing, but that stream carries a noise floor about 50 dB above the datasheet value and no measurable acoustic signal, and no MDF setting changes either fact. This is not a one-off. Two other STM32N6570-DK owners report noise or barely audible capture from the onboard microphone with ST's own code, with no root cause and no ST acknowledgement, while three other parties record clean speech from the same microphone with the same filter topology this bench already reproduced. The configuration differences between ST's packages (net gain, 16-bit extraction, cache placement of the DMA buffer, clock source, sleep clocks) explain "faint" or "garbage" captures elsewhere, but each one is either already matched here or cannot remove acoustic response. What remains is a microphone whose transducer or analogue front end does not deliver sound, a supply problem on VDD_MIC that has not been measured, or an undocumented state of the part. A fault at U13 is the best-supported explanation but is not proven: no test has yet isolated the part, the documented damage signatures do not match a steady Gaussian floor exactly, and the same symptom class on other boards means "this board was damaged" and "some DKs ship like this" are both open. Three tests costing minutes (re-run ST's example, measure VDD_MIC, repeat the external-microphone A/B on today's firmware) separate these.

## Two DK owners hit the same wall, three parties record speech

The public record splits cleanly. On the failing side, grashnar (DK "Rev B", STM32CubeN6 v1.3.0) got a zero-filled buffer from ST's MDF_AudioRecorder, "just noise" from STM32N6-GettingStarted-Audio, and "random noise which changes every callback" from direct BSP calls, with every API returning OK; the thread ends by asking whether anyone has captured audio from the onboard microphone at all, and nobody answered ([ST Community 159675](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mic-and-screen-setup-159675)). velfinnix (FW V1.4.1, BSP audio functions, caches disabled) hears "mostly noise", gets a barely audible response to tapping and has to "actually blow into it", while the same code on an STM32H7S78-DK picks up sound from several metres; the only ST reply points back to MDF_AudioRecorder ([ST Community 167402](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6-nucleo-board-mdf-issue-mics-records-noise-167402)). The same thread contains a NUCLEO-N657 user with an external MP23DB01HPTR, the same part number, who also records "a lot of noise". None of these threads reached a root cause, and no ST employee has acknowledged a problem with the DK microphone, the BSP or the example.

On the working side, vedantgg records usable audio from the onboard microphone at about 2.08 MHz with Sinc4, decimation 32, reshape /4 and gain +2 ([ST Community 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)). The fotonik speech recogniser trained on **248 hours recorded through this board's microphone** and works within about one metre ([fotonik README](https://github.com/fotonik-sk/stm32n6-speech-recognition/blob/main/README.md)). The upstream Zephyr MDF driver, merged for 4.5 and therefore absent from 4.4.0, "now records what is said to it" on a DK after a clamp bug in sample conversion was fixed ([zephyr PR 118736](https://github.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/pull/118736)). None of the working parties applied a hardware step, a start-up delay or a special clock sequence, and none states a board revision, so nobody is known to have succeeded or failed on MB1939 C02 specifically.

Two details from these threads bear directly on the bench. First, vedantgg's working board gave "mostly silence (about -71 dBFS) with bursts" at a 1.024 MHz PDM clock, inside the datasheet's low-power range ([ST Community 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)). Second, no report anywhere mentions SB19, VDD_MIC, U14, MIC_DET, a covered sound port, a revision difference or a defective batch; the hardware guesses in the forum came from a non-ST member who thought a broken MEMS "very unlikely" without having measurements.

## One filter topology everywhere; the differences do not reach this board

Every implementation that records from U13 uses MDF1 filter 0, CCK0 on PE2, SDI0 on PE8, the falling-edge bitstream, Sinc4 with the reshape filter at /4 and the high-pass at 0.000625 x fPCM. They differ in gain, in which 16 bits of the 24-bit result they keep, in clock source, and in memory placement of the DMA target. The "net gain" column below is derived here from the bench's own full-scale calibration (a full-scale bitstream reads -5.0 dB re 2^23 at SCALE +3.5 dB for a CIC gain of 2^20) plus the SCALE codes and extraction steps in the cited code; it is arithmetic, not a published figure.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Three conclusions follow. The gain story told in the forum is backwards for the BSP: because it keeps the low 16 bits of the 24-bit sample, the BSP has the highest net gain of any ST path, not the lowest, so a healthy -41 dBFS microphone would put 70 dB SPL speech near -19 dBFS in a BSP stream and its self-noise near -61 dBFS. A BSP user who hears "mostly noise" therefore has a microphone whose noise floor is far above the datasheet, which is exactly what this bench measures: the 7,000 to 8,500 LSB floor is about -55.5 dB re the calibrated full scale, and it would appear at about -10 dBFS through the BSP and about -22 dBFS through ST's example. The forum's "loud random noise" and this board's historical "weak audio with hiss" are what that floor sounds like through those gains. This is an inference from one board's measurement applied to other people's descriptions, but it is the first quantitative account that fits both.

Second, ST's application teams quietly correct the stock BSP in ways that explain garbage captures on other setups and are already handled here. Every ST application that runs with the data cache on moves the MDF DMA buffer and list node to non-cacheable memory, and all of them switch the audio PLL from HSE to HSI; the stock BSP does neither and also enables MDF1 and GPDMA1 without their sleep-enable bits ([GS-Audio patched BSP](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/STM32N6-GettingStarted-Audio/blob/v2.3.0/Projects/Common/Patch/stm32n6570_discovery_audio.c), [audio_bm.c](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/STM32N6-GettingStarted-Audio/blob/v2.3.0/Projects/GS/Src/audio_bm.c)). The bench uses AHB SRAM with explicit cache maintenance, has the sleep clocks on, tried three kernel clock sources and delivers the expected sample rate, so none of these applies.

Third, the bench has already run the working recipe, with a negative result. At about 2.08 MHz with Sinc4, decimation 32, reshape /4, a processing clock 12 times the bit clock, normal SPI and the falling edge (vedantgg's and fotonik's configuration in every respect the microphone can observe), the output was still **7,000 to 8,200 LSB rms Gaussian noise with no change during a loud 1 kHz tone**. A microphone sees only its supply, its clock and sound; the clock is verified at the pin, so no remaining firmware difference has a path to U13.

## The stream is valid, the floor is 50 dB high, and gain cannot hide a tap

The datasheet agrees with every electrical observation and disagrees with every acoustic one. With L/R at VDD the part drives DOUT while the clock is high, valid at most 120 ns after the rising edge, and releases it 4 to 17 ns after the falling edge ([DS13336 Rev 4, Tables 5 and 7](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)). The schematic straps L/R to VDD_MIC through 10 k ([MB1939 C02 schematic, sheet 16](https://www.st.com/resource/en/schematic_pack/mb1939-n6570-c02-schematic.pdf)). The measured 80 to 120 ns delay, the single bitstream held through the low phase, the identical result on both edges and the released line with a static clock are all normal behaviour for a lone microphone. The stream can only come from U13: UM3300 states that U14 routes U13 to PE8 unless a daughterboard is plugged into CN5 ([UM3300, section 7.15.2](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um3300-discovery-kit-with-stm32n657x0-mcu-stmicroelectronics.pdf)), and a floating or random line would read roughly 40 dB louder than the observed floor (the in-band share of a white bitstream, derived here) while a stuck line would read silence.

The levels are where the part departs from its specification. Performance mode specifies **-41 dBFS at 94 dB SPL and 65 to 65.5 dB(A) SNR**, which puts self-noise near -106 dBFS and full scale at 135 dB SPL ([DS13336 Rev 4, Table 4](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)). The measured floor of about -55.5 dBFS is therefore 45 to 50 dB above specification and equals the output a healthy part would give in an 80 dB SPL sound field. Its shape is wrong too: the datasheet's noise density falls 35 to 40 dB between 20 Hz and 1 kHz and is flat above 3 kHz, whereas the bench sees a uniform 2 dB per octave. The tone limit is equally stark. Below -92 dBFS re 2^23 is below -87 dB re the calibrated full scale, the level a healthy part produces at 48 dB SPL; a tone the owner heard across the room exceeds that at the board by tens of decibels. Tapping near the part is the strongest observation, because it is structure-borne, far louder than any speaker test, and independent of gain: gain scales signal and noise together, and the coherent analysis was looking 30 dB below the noise.

The low-power result needs careful reading. At 768 kHz the datasheet promises a live stream with **-24 dBFS sensitivity**, 17 dB hotter than performance mode, and self-noise near -88 dBFS(A) ([DS13336 Rev 4, Table 3](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)). The bench reads about -114 dBFS (9 LSB rms) at 768 kHz on either edge and at 1.024 MHz, unchanged throughout a loud tone, some 26 dB quieter than a healthy part's own noise, while the pin still toggles with normal statistics. A modulator that is quieter than its transducer's noise has no transducer signal at its input in that mode. Taken alone this looks damning, but vedantgg's board, which records speech at 2 MHz, also went nearly silent at 1.024 MHz. Low-power-range silence therefore does not discriminate a faulty unit from a working one on this board type; it shows that DK microphones do not follow the datasheet below 1.1 MHz, for a reason nobody has explained. The datasheet documents only three start conditions (20 ms wake-up, 40 to 60 % duty, and a clock that is continuous or stopped for at least 50 us across a mode change) and describes no latched bad state ([DS13336 Rev 4, Table 2](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20221107/4C24FD81CFC68EA40ADD0BE564FB9722.pdf)).

The MDF documentation closes the peripheral side. The measured full scale of 4.74 million matches the reference manual's gain chain (CIC gain 2^20, SCALE +3.5 dB, reshape filter about +9.5 dB) to within 0.1 dB, so the filter is doing what RM0486 says. Each documented way for the MDF to run without flags and deliver wrong audio has a different signature: CIC wrap-around needs strong signals and Sinc5/16 uses 21 of 26 bits, a reshape overrun sets RFOVRF, a ratio violation was swept through, and in normal SPI mode the serial interface is clocked from the pad, so a correct output rate proves the clock reaches PE2 ([RM0486, chapter 35](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/rm0486-stm32n6x5x7xx-armbased-32bit-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf)). AN5795 and the manual size SCALE for a full-scale bitstream and never mention microphone sensitivity, which is why users of this -41 dBFS part see low levels; ST staff confirm there is "no official configuration" and that the MP23 family is not recommended for new designs ([ST Community 168802](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/stm32n6570-dk-mp23db01hp-on-mdf1-recommended-cic-gain-hpf-pdm-clock-settings-for-16-khz-audio-and-start-up-thump-168802)). Low level is a real trap on this board, and it is not this board's problem.

## The case for a hardware fault is strong on location, weak on mechanism

**For a fault at U13 or its immediate environment.** Every layer the firmware controls has been verified first-hand: clock frequency and duty at the pin, data timing, the same bitstream through GPIO and through the MDF, and an absolute scale calibration. The two failures that remain, excess noise and absent sensitivity, are properties of the stream itself and survive every configuration change. The same pins and MDF gave clean audio with an external microphone. ST's unmodified example already produced "weak audio with hiss" on this board (owner's notes). ST's manufacturing note names this pair of symptoms: sensitivity reveals a damaged membrane, "in which case the THD, as well as the noise floor, is out of specification", and a noisy output "is typically due to contamination of the MEMS membrane" ([AN4428](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00103202.pdf), read as excerpts only). An ST engineer describes flux reaching the MEMS as causing "very unpredictable failure modes" and a part that is "definitely compromised" ([ST Community, MEMS contamination](https://community.st.com/t5/mems-sensors/mems-microphones-and-contamination/m-p/470216)). A dead transducer does not stop the modulator, which lives in the ASIC and runs from the clock, so a valid stream is exactly what a deaf part produces.

**Against concluding it now.** No test has isolated U13: VDD_MIC has not been measured, the port has not been inspected, and the external-microphone result comes from a different time, firmware and filter setting. The vendor literature describes sensitivity loss and noise that is intermittent, grows, or appears with sound; none of it describes a steady Gaussian floor 50 dB up with zero response, and a simply disconnected sensor should leave the ASIC quieter, not louder. The acoustic stimuli were uncalibrated. The owner's notes record tapping as audible through ST's example, and the owner recalls the microphone working once; if both are right, something changed, and whether that was the part or the test is unknown. Finally, two other owners describe the same symptom class with ST's code, so the fault, if it is one, may belong to a population of boards (part lot or assembly process) and not to anything done to this one. That last point cuts both ways: it weakens "this unit was damaged in handling" and strengthens "the hardware, not the firmware".

The defensible position is that the cause sits between VDD_MIC, U13 and its sound port, with the part itself the leading candidate, and that the firmware is cleared to the extent a microphone can be affected by firmware at all.

## Ranked explanations and the cheapest test for each

| Rank | Explanation | Evidence for | Evidence against | Cheapest test and reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | U13's transducer or analogue front end does not deliver sound (contaminated, stuck or ruptured membrane, or damaged input stage); unique to this board or shared by a lot is open | Valid modulator stream; floor 45 to 50 dB over specification with the wrong spectral shape; no response to tone or tap in any configuration; external microphone clean on the same pins; other DKs show noise-dominated capture through ST's code | No published signature matches a steady Gaussian floor; part never tested in isolation; earlier notes say tapping was audible | (a) Flash ST's unmodified MDF_AudioRecorder and listen: hiss with no tap response means the part has changed since the earlier note; tap still audible reopens rank 5. (b) Repeat the external-microphone A/B today on CN5 with unchanged firmware and performance-range clock; a STEVAL-MIC008A carries the same part number ([ST Community 158093](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/interleave-audio-by-connecting-steval-mic008a-dual-mems-to-stm32n6570-dk-cn5-158093)). Sound from the external part through the same clock and firmware convicts U13 |
| 2 | VDD_MIC is wrong (SB19 open or resistive, part back-fed through its clock pin or pull-ups, heavy ripple), starving the analogue front end while the digital side runs | Never measured; would produce a running modulator with a degraded front end; SB19's closed default rests on the schematic drawing alone | Stream timing and logic levels are in specification; nobody has reported it; inferred here, not documented | DMM on VDD_MIC at CN5 pin 2, 12 or 19 with the clock running and again with CCK0 held low. Expect 3.3 V in both; a rail that collapses when the clock stops means back-feeding. Scope it for ripple; check MIC_DET (CN5 pin 10) sits at VDD_MIC. A camera module is now attached (not on the microphone pins); it is a new load on the board supply, so repeat the reading and one capture with it unplugged. If the camera was absent when ST's example gave hiss, it cannot explain that result |
| 3 | The part sits in an undocumented state or mode (clock start, mode-change rule, behaviour outside the two specified ranges) | Low-power range contradicts the datasheet on this board and on a working one; the 1.1 to 1.5 MHz band is unspecified | Performance range swept from 1.5 to 3.2 MHz including ST's exact settings; many power cycles; no latched state is documented | Cold power cycle, start the clock once at 2.4 MHz, never change it, discard 20 ms, capture. Stop the clock for milliseconds across any mode change. If still deaf, this is closed. Optional: replace SB19 with a small shunt and read supply current against the datasheet (about 1 mA at 3.07 MHz, 0.35 mA at 768 kHz, microamps static) |
| 4 | Sound port obstructed (solder, flux, film or label over the PCB hole) as the sole cause | Bottom-port part with a 0.5 mm PCB hole; airborne tone absent | Does not raise the noise floor; does not block a tap | Inspect the hole on the face opposite U13 under magnification. Do not blow air into it, wash it or probe it ([InvenSense AN-100](https://www.cdiweb.com/datasheets/invensense/MEMS-Microphone-Handling-Guide.pdf), excerpt only) |
| 5 | A residual firmware or system difference from ST's bare-metal example | ST's example once gave some acoustic response on this board | Bitstream identical with and without the MDF; scale calibrated; working recipe reproduced; a microphone sees only supply, clock and sound | Covered by test 1(a) |

Run them in the order 2, 1(a), 1(b): a voltmeter reading, one flash, one jumpered microphone. MDF configuration proper (gain, edge, serial mode, clock ratio, filter order, kernel clock) is not in the table because the bench has excluded it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing more than the verdict. The bench had already shown that this board's microphone is deaf and noisy at the pin; the public record shows that other DKs behave the same way through ST's own code, that the BSP's high net gain makes "it is just quiet" untenable as the general explanation, and that the units which work do so with settings this board has already been given. The open question has narrowed from "which MDF setting is wrong" to "is U13 degraded, underpowered, or in a state the datasheet does not describe", and each branch has a test that costs minutes and no parts.

If the A/B test convicts U13, the measurements in hand (valid bitstream, calibrated full scale, floor at -55 dBFS, no response below -87 dBFS, MB1939 C02) would be the first documented case of this failure with pin-level evidence, which none of the unanswered forum threads supplied. That is worth posting to ST regardless of how this board is repaired, because it would turn three anecdotes into a question ST can answer: whether a lot of MP23DB01HP parts or a DK assembly step leaves some boards with microphones that clock out noise and hear nothing.
