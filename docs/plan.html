The NPU on the camera picture: hands, then faces

The Camera screen (IMX335 at 30 fps, ST's ISP) gets two recognition modes, run on the N6's Neural-ART NPU: hand tracking with gestures (any orientation, either hand), then face detection with recognition of faces it has been taught. Hands come first. The numbers below come from converting each model with ST Edge AI 4.0 for this chip, with its working memory placed in PSRAM; times on the board are measured in step 1.

The four Google models are the ones ST ships in its N6 hand and face applications. SFace as published ran almost half its steps on the CPU, because the NPU has no PReLU activation: each PReLU is rewritten exactly as a·x + (1−a)·ReLU(x) from operators the NPU runs (outputs equal to within 10−6), then the model is re-quantized to per-channel int8. The few CPU steps left in each network are input scaling and quantization at its edges.

Hands

  • The palm detector finds hands and their rotation; the landmark model places 21 points on each, from a crop the CPU rotates upright.
  • Gestures are ours, from joint angles, so they hold at any rotation and for either hand: fingers extended, finger count, point, thumbs up, V, OK, fist, open palm. Fingertip paths are tracked over time.
  • On screen: the skeleton over the live picture, the gesture's name, each network's time on the NPU.

Faces

  • Every frame: BlazeFace, then the 468-point mesh for each face (precise eyes for alignment; the mesh can be drawn).
  • Identification when a face first appears, about once a second to confirm, and when a better view arrives. Faces are followed between frames, so names stay on them.
  • LEARN takes about five good aligned views and averages their signatures; matching is cosine similarity against a threshold calibrated on public same-person and different-person pairs. Below it: "unknown".
  • Quantization is calibrated with public face crops kept on the PC; the int8 model is checked against the float one (signature agreement, verification accuracy) before it reaches the board.

Risks

ST's forum has an unresolved report of NPU bus faults when camera, display and NPU run together on the N6570-DK, which is this combination: the soak test in N4 is there for it. Working memory in PSRAM turned out 14 to 16 times slower than in the SRAM banks (step N1), so the NPU borrows the second frame buffer while it runs; a camera frame read by the CPU for the crop costs about 10 ms, which may hold tracking below the camera's 30 fps.

Built 2026-11-17

A theremin on Signal Lab: the spectrum analyser you can play

Signal Lab gets a third source next to the generator and the microphone: THEREMIN. With it selected, the spectrum becomes the instrument. A fingertip on it sounds a tone whose frequency is the spectrum's own frequency at that column (the same log axis, 20 Hz to 20 kHz) and whose level is the spectrum's own dB value at that height. What the analyser then shows is the tone it is playing, so the peak sits under the finger, follows it when it slides, and drops with it. The scope above shows the waveform, the waterfall below writes the melody as it scrolls. Up to five fingers play five voices, a chord shows as five peaks.

Playing

  • Horizontal: frequency, sig_col_hz(col), so the tone lands exactly on the axis under the finger. The field stops 28 px from the screen edges, where the shell's edge swipes start, so changing screens still works; that costs only 20 to 23 Hz and 18 to 20 kHz.
  • Vertical: level, the inverse of the spectrum's db_to_y: the top is -6 dBFS (a cap, for ears and headphones), the bottom silence. In this mode the spectrum takes most of the waterfall's height (about 270 px instead of 146: about 2 px a dB), and the waterfall stays as a strip that draws the trail.
  • Each finger gets a crosshair through spectrum and waterfall and a small readout beside it: note name, frequency, level ("A4 440.0 Hz -18 dB"), drawn in the pane's draw handler, not as label objects.
  • A WAVE label cycles the timbre, each with its own picture on the log spectrum: sine (one peak), theremin (a sine slightly overdriven, with a little vibrato), triangle (odd harmonics falling 12 dB an octave), sawtooth (every harmonic, band-limited so nothing folds back).
  • A SNAP label (off, semitones): the pitch glides to the nearest semitone, and faint note ticks (C2 to C8) appear on the axis.

Sound

  • A synthesizer in dsp/theremin.c is the audio output's source (audio_set_source, 512 frames every 10.7 ms): five phase-continuous oscillators, so a frequency change never steps the waveform.
  • Every change is a ramp, per the rule that the sound never steps: pitch glides in log frequency (about 8 ms), level follows in about 5 ms, a touch fades in over 10 ms, a lift fades out over about 150 ms (the theremin's decay). Voices are summed through a soft limiter, so five loud fingers never clip.
  • The synthesizer reads the touch state itself at the start of each block (touch_get() from the audio thread), not through the UI frame: the touch reaches the sound in one to two blocks, 11 to 21 ms after the panel reports it. The screen shows this as a measured touch to sound figure.
  • Every sample it plays also goes into a ring that Signal Lab analyses as its source, so the picture is the sound that reaches the DAC.
  • The DAC's clock runs at 47.7 kHz, not 48 (measured, PLL2 from the HSI): the oscillators and the analysis both use the measured rate, so A4 is 440 Hz in the air and on the axis.

Planned 2026-11-17 · for later

A video of one attract lap, with an explainer for builders

A video for YouTube, about four to five minutes: one attract lap (about 2 min 20 s) exactly as the panel shows it, frame for frame and with the board's own sound, carried by an explainer that tells other builders what is in it and what they can take into their own projects. The lap is the real demo at real speed, live data and 60 fps, not a re-enactment.

Capturing it: the board records itself

  • The N6 has a hardware H.264 encoder (VENC, VeriSilicon VC8000NanoE). Zephyr 4.4 has a driver for it (video_stm32_venc), and ST's encoder library is BSD-3-Clause, fetched by west with the HAL: nothing to keep out of the repository.
  • The library reads 32-bit RGB directly (H264ENC_RGB888, red in bits 23 to 16: this panel's frame buffer layout), so the frame just presented is its input, with no conversion and no copy. Zephyr's driver only offers NV12 and RGB565 input; the app carries its own small front end to the library instead, and the Zephyr tree stays unmodified.
  • Timing: a presented buffer stays untouched for one refresh (16.7 ms) before LVGL draws into it again, and the encoder has to finish within it. ST rates the encoder for full-HD camera video, about five times this panel's pixels a frame, so every other frame (30 fps) has room and every frame (60 fps) may: measured in V1.
  • The sound is the DAC feed itself (what acap records for 3 s today), streamed with the pictures on the same clock: bit-exact, no analog cable, no drift between sound and picture.
  • With every frame the board sends its number, present time, render time and screen, so the video can carry a frame-time strip computed from the board's own measurements.
  • One client on a new port (tcp/5559), like the screenshot port; off, and free, when nobody records. A board that records its own screen is also something builders can reuse, for documentation and bug reports.

Why Zephyr + LVGL

Zephyr 4.4.0 already describes this board's display, touch, Ethernet, PSRAM, and flash in its devicetree, and the workspace is on this machine and known to boot. LVGL 9.5 supplies layout, text, and input handling with Helium-accelerated blending, which leaves the effort for custom drawing where it shows.

What was rejected

  • TouchGFX. Strong on this chip, but it is driven from a GUI designer that cannot be scripted from here, and it is not installed.
  • Bare-metal Cube HAL. Every peripheral example exists, but networking, threading, and the build would be rebuilt by hand.
  • Software H.264. The chip has an H.264 encoder but no decoder. A software decoder at 480p30 is unproven on this core; the hardware JPEG codec is the safe path.
Display

Frame budget and composition

The LTDC blends two planes in hardware. Video decodes into the lower plane; the UI renders into the upper plane with per-pixel alpha. Player controls fade over moving video without the CPU touching a video pixel. Buffer swaps happen only in vertical blanking, so nothing tears.

One frame at 60 fps16.7 ms
Input + app logic 1.0 ms LVGL render 8.0 ms Custom viz draw 3.0 ms Slack before vsync 4.7 ms

These are targets, not measurements. Phase 0 measures fill and blit rates with framebuffers in PSRAM and in internal SRAM, then fixes the pixel format. Preferred outcome: 32-bit buffers for band-free gradients. Fallback: dithered RGB565 double buffer in internal SRAM. The performance HUD and the Flight Deck show the measured frame-time distribution, including the worst frame.

Video

The panel is 800 x 480, so the ceiling is 480p class. The plan is full-screen 800 x 480 at 30 fps with 48 kHz stereo sound, instant scrubbing, and no visible compression artifacts. Method

Build loop and verification

Getting code onto the board

The N6 boot ROM picks its source from the BOOT1 switch, and the proven flash path needs development-boot mode. To avoid asking you to flip a switch on every build, Phase 0 tries leaving the board in development-boot mode: write the app to XSPI flash over SWD, then start MCUboot from RAM through the debugger. Once Ethernet works, updates go over the network instead. You flip BOOT1 once at the end for stand-alone power-up.

How I check a screen I cannot see

  • Framebuffer readback. Screenshots over Ethernet after every build, compared against the previous good frame.
  • Host simulator. The same UI code compiled for the PC renders reference frames without hardware.
  • On-board counters. Frame-time percentiles, dropped frames, decode times, logged over serial and HTTP.
  • Host tests. DSP, decimation, and stream parsing run as unit tests on the PC.

What still needs your eyes and ears

Readback proves what was rendered, not what the panel shows. Tearing, motion smoothness, panel color, touch feel, and audio quality need a person. Each phase ends with a short checklist for you, five items or fewer.

Phases and exit criteria

Each phase ends in a build that runs on the board. Phase 0 exists to retire the risks before any screen is designed.

PhaseWorkDone when
P0 SpikesWest workspace, MCUboot + app boot, color bars, touch coordinates, DHCP and ping, hands-free load, framebuffer readback, memory bake-off, one hardware-decoded JPEG frame.A screenshot of the panel arrives on the host over Ethernet, and the fill-rate table picks the pixel format.
P1 PlatformTwo-plane display driver with vsync swap, LVGL integration, frame pacing, telemetry, design tokens, fonts, components, screen manager, host simulator.Two placeholder screens swap with a spring transition at a measured 60 fps; simulator frames match the board.
P2 Core screensHome, Flight Deck, Touch Lab, performance HUD.Worst frame under 16.7 ms across a 10 minute interaction log.
P3 Data vizSignal Lab (microphone, FFT, waterfall, scope) and Explorer (LOD pyramid, pan, zoom, cursors).A played test tone lands in the correct FFT bin; a full-range zoom on one million samples holds frame rate.
P4 NetworkHost companion, Host Link screen, web remote, firmware update over UDP.A phone on the LAN switches screens; a new build installs with no cable touched.
P5 VideoStream protocol, decode path, A/V sync, Cinema UI, offline loop in flash.Ten minutes of 480p30 with sound: no dropped frames, audio offset within 40 ms.
P6 PolishAttract mode, motion tuning, error states (cable pulled, host gone), soak test, stand-alone boot, README and project notes.An 8 hour unattended run with no reset, no leak, and no frame-time drift.
P7 StretchCamera preview with NPU object detection as a ninth screen, if the camera module is fitted and you want it.Agreed separately.
Risk

What could go wrong, and the fallback

RiskLevelFallback
Hands-free load failsmediumAn earlier attempt on this board to flash without the switch did not work. If the RAM-start route also fails, you flip BOOT1 per flash until network update is up.
PSRAM too slow for 32-bit framesmediumRGB565 double buffer in internal SRAM with ordered dithering on gradients.
JPEG decode pathmediumZephyr 4.4.0's JPEG driver only encodes, so decode calls the ST HAL directly, modelled on ST's example. If hardware decode misbehaves, drop to half resolution in software.
Audio output wiringmediumThe codec and SAI drivers exist upstream but this board's devicetree does not connect them. If it stalls, video ships silent with an on-screen level meter and audio becomes a follow-up.
MCUboot execute-in-place on N6lowDocumented upstream as the default. Alternative is RAM-load mode, which also frees the flash for runtime writes.
LVGL too slow for full-screen motionlowHeavy visuals already bypass LVGL and draw straight into the buffer. The NeoChrom GPU library ships in the LVGL tree as optional acceleration, off the critical path.
Ethernet throughputlowVideo needs under 2 percent of a gigabit link. If the driver stalls under load, lower the bitrate or play from flash.
ScopemediumEight screens is a lot. The order above ships the strongest material first; Explorer and Host Link are the ones to cut if time runs short.
